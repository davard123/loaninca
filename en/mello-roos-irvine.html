What Is Mello-Roos? How It Works in Irvine and Orange County, and How It Affects Your Mortgage

Mello-Roos is a special tax levied by a Community Facilities District (CFD) under California's Mello-Roos Community Facilities Act of 1982 (Government Code §53311 and following). In Orange County it is collected on your regular property tax bill and listed under "Special Assessment Charges", according to the OC Treasurer-Tax Collector. Lenders count it as part of your monthly housing payment under Fannie Mae Selling Guide B3-6-03, so when your DTI is at the limit, each dollar of Mello-Roos is a dollar less for principal and interest.

Many newer neighborhoods in Irvine, and in other parts of south Orange County, were built with CFD financing. The district sells bonds to pay for streets, parks, schools, sewers or other facilities, and property owners in the district repay those bonds through the special tax. If you are shopping for a newer home, assume there may be a CFD until the documents say otherwise.

How Mello-Roos shows up on the Orange County tax bill

The OC Treasurer-Tax Collector explains that CFD bond charges appear in the Special Assessment Charges section of the secured property tax bill, now listed under the actual name of the CFD bond. The Treasurer directs CFD questions to the phone number shown for the bond on the bill. That agency, not the Tax Collector, can tell you how the tax is calculated and when it ends. The Treasurer's page also links to a Mello-Roos tax map.

How a CFD tax can change over time

Each CFD has its own rate and method set when the district was formed, so read the district's documents rather than relying on a neighbor's number. As one Irvine example, the City of Irvine page for CFD No. 2013-3 (Great Park) says the charge can increase by up to 2% per year, and that once the original bonds are repaid (which the City says typically takes 40 years) the charge drops substantially while a smaller portion continues for maintenance. Other districts work differently.

What the seller has to disclose

Under California Civil Code §1102.6b, a seller of property subject to a continuing Mello-Roos lien must make a good-faith effort to get a disclosure notice from the agency that levies the tax and give it to the buyer. A notice from a private source must state the annual tax for the current year, the maximum tax that can be levied, the maximum annual increase, and the date until which the tax may be levied. (The official leginfo site blocks automated access; this page links to the california.public.law mirror of the statute.)

How lenders count Mello-Roos in your DTI

DTI (debt-to-income ratio) is your monthly debt payments divided by your gross monthly income. Fannie Mae B3-6-03 defines the monthly housing expense to include principal and interest, insurance, real estate taxes, special assessments and owners' association dues. Mello-Roos is a special assessment for this purpose, and HOA dues are counted next to it. For a purchase, the lender must use a reasonable estimate of the real estate taxes based on the land and all improvements, not the seller's old bill. Under B3-6-02, the maximum DTI for a loan underwritten through Fannie Mae's Desktop Underwriter (DU) is 50%; manually underwritten loans start at 36%.

When your DTI is at the limit, the monthly payment you can carry is fixed. Mello-Roos does not change that ceiling; it uses part of it. The loan amount drops by the Mello-Roos amount divided by the monthly payment per $1,000 borrowed.

What to do before you write an offer

  • Ask the listing agent or builder for the Mello-Roos disclosure and the most recent tax bill for the parcel.
  • Look at the Special Assessment Charges section and call the number printed for each CFD. Ask for the current annual tax, the maximum tax, the annual escalator and the end date.
  • Check whether there is more than one HOA (master plus sub-association) and add every monthly due.
  • Give your lender the CFD amount up front so the pre-approval uses the full payment, not just principal, interest, taxes and insurance.
  • Ask a CPA whether any part of the special tax is deductible for you. This page does not answer tax questions.

Worked example

This example uses made-up numbers to show the math. It is not a real client. Rate: 6.5% on a 30-year fixed loan, assumed for illustration only; not a quote and not a current market rate.

Payment per $1,000 borrowed = 1,000 × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)−360), where r = 6.5% ÷ 12 = 0.005417. That is about $6.32 per $1,000.

Assume gross income of $18,000 a month, a 50% DTI limit, and $1,000 a month of other debts. Total allowed debt is $9,000, so $8,000 is left for housing.

  • Home A, no CFD: property tax and insurance $1,500 + HOA $250 = $1,750. Principal and interest available: $8,000 − $1,750 = $6,250. Loan ≈ $6,250 ÷ $6.32 × 1,000 ≈ $988,800.
  • Home B, same costs plus $500 a month of Mello-Roos: $2,250 of non-loan costs, leaving $5,750. Loan ≈ $909,700.

The $500 a month of Mello-Roos ($6,000 a year) cuts the loan amount by about $79,100 ($500 ÷ $6.32 × 1,000). At an assumed 6% rate, each $100 a month of Mello-Roos is worth about $16,700 of loan amount; at an assumed 7% rate, about $15,000. Figures are rounded.

Frequently asked questions

Is Mello-Roos included in my property tax bill?

In Orange County, yes. The OC Treasurer-Tax Collector collects Mello-Roos on the regular secured property tax bill and lists it under Special Assessment Charges with the name of the CFD bond.

Is Mello-Roos paid monthly or annually?

The tax is billed with your property taxes, which in Orange County come in two installments. If your loan has an impound (escrow) account, the lender collects a monthly amount for taxes, including Mello-Roos, and pays the bill for you.

Does Mello-Roos ever go away?

It depends on the district. Each CFD sets its own terms. For Irvine's Great Park CFD, the City says the charge drops after the original bonds are repaid but a smaller portion continues for maintenance. Ask the agency named on your tax bill for the end date.

Is Mello-Roos tax deductible?

That is a tax question that depends on how the levy is structured and your own return. Ask a CPA before you count on a deduction.

If my DTI is under the limit, does Mello-Roos still matter?

It will not change whether you are approved, but it is still a real monthly cost. Compare homes on the full payment: principal, interest, taxes, Mello-Roos, HOA and insurance.
