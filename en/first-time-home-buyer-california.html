First-Time Home Buyer in California (2026): Who Qualifies, 3% Down, FHA and CalHFA

For most programs, you are a first-time home buyer if you have not owned a home in the past three years; Fannie Mae applies that test through the Form 1003 declarations (B2-1.3-01), and CalHFA adds that you also cannot have lived in a home your spouse owned in that period (CalHFA MyHome handbook). The main low-down-payment paths are a conventional loan with 3% down, FHA, and CalHFA's MyHome assistance of up to 3% or 3.5% of the price. CalHFA's 2026 income limit for Orange County is $274,000 (Dream For All: $219,000), effective June 30, 2026 (CalHFA income limits).

Who counts as a first-time buyer

  • Fannie Mae: for loans above 95% loan-to-value, at least one borrower must be a first-time homebuyer, shown by answering "No" on the Form 1003 question about owning a property interest in the last three years.
  • HUD: the HOME program statute, 42 U.S.C. §12704(14), defines a first-time homebuyer as someone (and spouse) who has not owned a home during the 3-year period before the purchase, and says a displaced homemaker or single parent cannot be excluded only because they owned a home with a spouse while married.
  • CalHFA: the MyHome handbook (revised October 19, 2026) defines a first-time homebuyer as a borrower with no ownership interest in a principal residence, and who has not lived in a home owned by a spouse, during the previous three years. For MyHome, every borrower must live in the home and meet that definition.

Path 1: conventional loan with 3% down

Fannie Mae allows loan-to-value up to 97% on a purchase only when all of these are true (B2-1.3-01): a one-unit principal residence; a fixed-rate loan with a term up to 30 years (no ARMs and no high-balance loans); all borrowers occupy the home; at least one borrower is a first-time homebuyer; and the loan is underwritten through DU. The Fannie Mae Eligibility Matrix has the full LTV grid by loan type.

HomeReady is Fannie Mae's program for lower-income buyers: qualifying income cannot exceed 80% of the area median income for the property's location, and homeownership education is required when all occupying borrowers are first-time buyers (B5-6-01). Below 20% down, conventional loans need mortgage insurance; ask for the monthly cost in writing.

Path 2: FHA

FHA is insured by HUD. Since HUD Mortgagee Letter 2025-09, FHA no longer accepts non-permanent residents: lenders had to apply the change to FHA case numbers assigned on or after May 25, 2025, so the borrower must be a U.S. citizen or a lawful permanent resident (the letter also keeps a narrow category for citizens of Micronesia, the Marshall Islands and Palau). If you are on a work visa, look at conventional loans instead.

Path 3: CalHFA programs

CalHFA is the California Housing Finance Agency. It does not take applications directly; you apply through a CalHFA-approved lender. David works with NDF Lending, a CalHFA-approved lender.

  • MyHome Assistance Program: a deferred-payment junior loan for down payment and/or closing costs, up to 3.5% of the price or appraised value (whichever is less) with a CalHFA FHA first mortgage, or up to 3% with a CalHFA conventional, VA or USDA first mortgage. Combined loan-to-value cannot exceed 105%. It is repaid when you sell, transfer title, refinance or pay off the first loan.
  • Eligibility: each borrower must be a U.S. citizen or national, or a "qualified alien" under 8 U.S.C. §1641; all borrowers must occupy the home within 60 days of closing; non-occupant co-borrowers are not allowed; homebuyer education is required; and income cannot exceed CalHFA's county limit.
  • 2026 income limits (effective June 30, 2026): Orange County $274,000 for CalHFA first mortgages and subordinate loans other than Dream For All; Los Angeles County and other counties are listed in the CalHFA income limit chart.
  • Dream For All: a shared appreciation loan of up to 20% of the price, capped at $150,000, repaid with a share of appreciation. One borrower must be a first-generation homebuyer. The Dream For All page says the application period is closed (the portal closed March 16, 2026), and its latest update, dated May 20, 2026, says the next round of vouchers has been released and asks applicants to check their portal status. No new application round was posted as of November 12, 2026. The Dream For All income limit for Orange County is $219,000 (chart).

What to do first

  • Decide the monthly payment you are comfortable with, including taxes, insurance, HOA and any Mello-Roos.
  • Gather two years of W-2s or tax returns, recent pay stubs and bank statements, and document where the down payment comes from.
  • Take a homebuyer education course early; CalHFA and some Fannie Mae loans require it before closing.
  • Ask a lender to compare 3% down conventional, FHA (if eligible) and CalHFA side by side using official Loan Estimates.

Worked example

This example uses made-up numbers to show how the rules fit together. It is not a real client and not an approval.

A married couple, both U.S. citizens, rent in Irvine and have never owned a home. Their qualifying income is $180,000 a year, and they are looking at a $700,000 condo. They are under CalHFA's $274,000 Orange County limit. If they use a CalHFA conventional first mortgage, MyHome could provide up to 3% × $700,000 = $21,000 toward the down payment and closing costs, as long as the combined loan-to-value stays at or below 105% and the first mortgage, the condo project and their credit meet CalHFA, lender and mortgage insurer rules. They would both need to move in within 60 days, and one of them would take the homebuyer education course. The MyHome loan would be repaid when they sell or refinance.

Frequently asked questions

Can I be a first-time home buyer if I owned a home before?

Usually yes, if you have not owned a home in the last three years. CalHFA also looks at whether you lived in a home your spouse owned during that time.

What is the minimum down payment for a first-time buyer in California?

Fannie Mae allows 3% down on a one-unit primary home with a fixed-rate loan when at least one borrower is a first-time buyer and the loan is not high-balance. CalHFA's MyHome can help cover part of the down payment for eligible buyers.

Can H-1B visa holders use FHA or CalHFA?

Not under current rules. FHA now requires U.S. citizenship or lawful permanent residence, and CalHFA's MyHome requires each borrower to be a citizen, national or qualified alien under 8 U.S.C. 1641. Conventional loans remain available to lawfully present non-permanent residents.

Is Dream For All open in 2026?

As of November 12, 2026, CalHFA's Dream For All page says the application period is closed. The portal closed March 16, 2026. Check calhfa.ca.gov for any new round.

Do I need a CalHFA-approved lender?

Yes. CalHFA does not accept applications directly. You apply through a CalHFA-approved lender, such as NDF Lending.
